Figure 1. Lung cancer mortality: demographic and geographic patterns in the United States. (A) Age-adjusted mortality rates (AAMRs) overall and by sex, 1999–2024. (B) Age-specific crude mortality rates (CMRs) by age group, 1999–2024. (C) AAMRs by race and ethnicity, 1999–2024; the dashed line marks the 2021 transition from Bridged Race to Single Race data. The Other category is not compositionally equivalent across this transition, so its two eras should not be read as one continuous series. (D) AAMRs by census region, 1999–2024. (E) AAMRs by metropolitan status, 1999–2020; the Bridged Race database used for this series ends in 2020. (F) The eight states with the highest pooled AAMRs, 2018–2024. These rankings are descriptive and were not formally compared. All rates are per 100,000 population.

Figure 2. Lung cancer mortality with co-recorded chronic kidney disease (CKD) in the United States. (A) Age-adjusted mortality rates (AAMRs) overall and by sex. (B) Age-specific crude mortality rates (CMRs) by age group; points mark valid annual observations, gaps shows suppressed or unreliable data. The 2015 rate for ages 45–54 years was flagged unreliable, omitted and not interpolated. (C) AAMRs by race and ethnicity; the vertical line marks the 2021 transition from Bridged Race to Single Race data. The Other category is not compositionally equivalent across this transition and its two eras should not be read as one continuous series. (D) AAMRs by census region. (E) Historical AAMRs by metropolitan status, through 2020 because the Bridged Race data ends in 2020. (F) The eight states with the highest pooled Lung+CKD AAMRs for 2018–2024; rankings are descriptive only. The shaded 2011–2012 interval in (A), (B), (D), and (E) marks the CKD coding transition; primary Lung+CKD trend analyses begin in 2013.

Figure 3. National crude and age-standardized CKD co-recording, 2013–2024. (A) Crude co-recording proportion and age-standardized predictions from the continuous-year quasi-binomial model. Crude proportions were calculated directly from annual death counts, whereas model-standardized proportions were estimated by averaging age-specific predictions using the same pooled 2013–2024 age distribution of lung-cancer decedents for every year. The shaded band is the dispersion-adjusted 95% confidence interval (CI). (B) Predictions from the continuous-year model and a categorical-year sensitivity model. The categorical-year model allows year-to-year variation without assuming a logit-linear trend. Both models are standardized to the same pooled 2013–2024 age distribution of lung cancer decedents. The co-recording proportion is not a mortality rate, is a percentage of lung-cancer deaths, and does not represent CKD prevalence among all patients with lung cancer.

Supplementary Figure S1. Age-standardized predicted CKD co-recording proportions by six-level 2023 NCHS urbanicity among lung-cancer decedents aged ≥55 years, 2018–2024. Predictions were standardized to a common pooled 2018–2024 age distribution (55–64, 65–74, 75–84, and ≥85 years). The global year-by-urbanicity interaction was not statistically significant (Wald P = .140). This analysis is presented as a sensitivity analysis; the lines should not be interpreted as formal pairwise superiority tests. “2023 NCHS” denotes the classification-scheme vintage, not the mortality-data endpoint. No formal evidence of differential trends was found (global year × urbanicity Wald P = .140). The lines are plotted in NCHS classification order, and their vertical ordering in 2024 must not be read as a ranking of urbanicity categories.
